## How the portfolio is actually shaped

Three of the six sit on one stretch of Hollywood Road in Atlanta's Westside: [[02 Portfolio/Dwell at Westside 3|Westside 3]], [[02 Portfolio/Dwell at Hollywood|Hollywood]], and [[02 Portfolio/Dwell at the View|the View]]. They share a single leasing email. Whether they share staff, budget, and reporting is one of the first things to find out, because it decides whether a procedure written for "a property" means a building or a cluster.

Two are separately branded and do not carry the Dwell name in front of residents: [[02 Portfolio/Ilion|Ilion]] and [[02 Portfolio/Clairmont Crest|Clairmont Crest]]. That was a positioning decision someone made on purpose. Find out why before touching either one's marketing or its Elise.ai configuration.

[[02 Portfolio/Clairmont Crest|Clairmont Crest]] is 55-plus. Age restriction changes fair-housing exposure, leasing cycle, and resident communication, so assume every portfolio-wide procedure needs a Clairmont exception until someone proves it doesn't.

[[02 Portfolio/Dwell at 750|Dwell at 750]] is the largest asset and also the company's headquarters address. It's the only property with published construction phasing, which makes it the clearest available window into how Dwell runs a capital project.

## Open questions

- Dwell's own LinkedIn says 7 properties and 1,027 units. The website lists 6, which sum to roughly 1,016. There is a seventh property and it isn't named anywhere public.
- The same LinkedIn copy claims Atlanta **and Houston**. No Texas asset turned up in any search. Either it's aspirational, stale, or unlisted.
- What does the business call the phases of a property? The schema currently uses `prospect` · `acquiring` · `renovating` · `stabilizing` · `operating` · `disposed`, and Dwell publishes no lifecycle language at all, so those six words are the system maintainer's guess. Ask, then correct them.
